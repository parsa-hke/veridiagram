\section{Model and Training}
\label{sec:model}

The backbone decides what the eye brings to the page. We use DINOv3 ViT-L/16 \cite{simeoni2025dinov3}, a vision transformer \cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} with 24 layers and four register tokens \cite{darcet2024registers}, trained by self-distillation on images alone. Two properties decided it over a convolutional \cite{he2016resnet} or caption-aligned \cite{radford2021clip} encoder: attention joins every patch to every other in every layer, and a relation joins two primitives that may sit at opposite corners of the image; and self-supervised features stay dense at the stroke level. DINOv2-L \cite{oquab2024dinov2} is run once under the same recipe. The registers are dropped, the CLS token is concatenated with the mean patch token, and a LayerNorm with one linear layer of six sigmoid outputs is the head, trained with a masked binary cross-entropy.

Training follows LP-FT \cite{kumar2022lpft}: the head is fitted on frozen features of \ftLPQueries{} queries, then the whole network is fine-tuned, because a random head distorts pre-trained features in the first steps and the damage shows out of distribution. The budget is fixed at \ftEpochs{} epochs of \ftEpochImages{} images, so a run with fifty thousand synthetic diagrams sees as many images as one with none; with synthetic data every batch is \ftRealFractionPct\% real. AdamW \cite{loshchilov2019adamw} with layer-wise decayed learning rates \cite{howard2018ulmfit}, a cosine schedule \cite{loshchilov2017sgdr}, \ftAmpDtype{} precision \cite{micikevicius2018amp}, gradient checkpointing \cite{chen2016checkpointing} and an exponential weight average \cite{polyak1992averaging}, selected at the best validation macro-F1. Fine-tunes run \nSeeds{} seeds; ablations one. The budget was set by a shared classroom GPU at \benchTrainImgPerS{} images per second; the thirty-epoch plan first written would have taken weeks.
