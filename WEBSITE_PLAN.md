# Project website: plan

A public web page for the project with three jobs: explain the problem, model and approach; show
the results; let anyone try the model on their own diagrams. This file is the brief for the session
that builds it. Read `CLAUDE.md` and `GEOMETRY_RELATIONS_PLAN.md` first; their rules apply here
too: every new module gets a test under 10 s, data shown on the page is generated by a script from
`runs/` and `reports/` (never typed in by hand), CHANGELOG.md is updated, and **commits are authored
by the user only: no Claude co-author line**.

---

## 1. The concept: "Colour, not Letters"

### 1.1 The idea

In 1847 Oliver Byrne published *The First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid* with almost no
letters: every line and angle is a block of **red, yellow, blue or black**, and the proofs refer to
the shapes by colour. It is one of the most famous unconventional books ever designed.

This project reached Byrne's conclusion by experiment. The model is asked about a pair of lines by
**painting** them in a highlight colour. When the same question is given with letters instead (the
no-overlay ablation, 7.8: "is AB parallel to CD?" as point-name tokens), macro-F1 falls from about
0.87 to 0.43, **below** a frozen linear probe that gets the colour. Colour beats letters.

So the site is designed as a **lost fifth edition of Byrne's Euclid, written for a machine**:
- cream paper, Caslon type, huge vermilion drop caps, Byrne's red / yellow / blue / black;
- the four sections are "Books", findings are "Propositions" that end in Q.E.D.;
- the model's own highlight colour, **green (0, 200, 0)**, is the one colour Byrne never used. We
  chose it because it appears in **0 of 600** sampled textbook diagrams, so it can never be confused
  with ink. On the page green belongs to the machine only: whenever something is green, the model is
  looking at it.

That gives the site a story, a look nobody else has, and a reason for every design choice.

### 1.2 What makes it jaw-dropping

1. **The page draws itself.** Every figure is an SVG construction drawn stroke by stroke as you
   scroll (compass arcs first, then the straightedge, then the fill), the way a geometer constructs
   it. No stock charts: the results are also constructions (bars are Byrne rectangles, the scaling
   curve is a polyline plotted by a compass).
2. **Three witnesses.** The demo never just prints a probability. For anything you draw, three
   verdicts appear side by side: **the Ruler** (the coordinate verifier, exact), **the Eye** (the
   vision transformer, from pixels only) and, when relevant, **the Label** (what a textbook would
   have marked). Their disagreements are the research question made tangible.
3. **You can beat the machine, or not.** A ten-round game of near-threshold diagrams: you, the
   model and the ruler each judge "parallel or not".
4. **"What the model sees."** Every answer shows the exact 448-px image the network received, green
   highlight included, next to your drawing. (Phase 8 adds its attention map on top.)
5. **Night edition.** Dark mode is not inverted paper but a blackboard: chalk-white lines and the
   same four Byrne colours, slightly desaturated.

### 1.3 Design system

| Token | Light (paper) | Dark (blackboard) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--paper` | `#F3EAD3` | `#1C2421` | page background |
| `--ink` | `#1A1A1A` | `#ECE8DC` | text, black lines |
| `--byrne-red` | `#C8312B` | `#E0574F` | first highlighted element, drop caps |
| `--byrne-yellow` | `#E8B10E` | `#F0C23A` | second element |
| `--byrne-blue` | `#1F4E8C` | `#5B8BD0` | third element |
| `--machine` | `#00C800` | `#00C800` | the model and nothing else |
| `--rule` | `#B9AE95` | `#3A4541` | hairlines, margins |

- **Type** (Google Fonts): `Libre Caslon Display` for titles, `Libre Caslon Text` for body,
  `IM Fell English SC` for small caps and proposition labels, `JetBrains Mono` only for numbers in
  the demo and API payloads. Old-style figures in running text.
- **Layout:** a book page. One wide text column with **margin notes** (Tufte-style sidenotes) for
  technical detail: layer-wise LR decay, tolerances, seeds. On phones the notes fold into tappable
  "†" markers. 16 px side gutter, no horizontal scroll.
- **Motion:** stroke-dashoffset drawing tied to scroll position; one construction per screen at
  most. `prefers-reduced-motion`: figures appear finished, no drawing.
- **Tone:** confident, precise, a little playful in the headings ("Proposition IV: That colour
  speaketh louder than letters"), plain English in the body. Every number has its seed count; single
  seeds are labelled as such.

## 2. Information architecture (one long page plus the demo)

### Frontispiece (hero)
A blank page; a compass draws a circle, a straightedge draws two lines, the lines fill red and
yellow (Byrne), then a **green** stroke traces both, and a small engraved caption reads
`parallel · 0.97`. Title: **"Colour, not Letters"**, subtitle: *Teaching a vision transformer to
read the geometry in textbook diagrams.* Two buttons: "Read the Elements" (scroll) and "Construct
something" (jump to the demo). Course line: MAAI 901, UOWD, author name.

### Book I: The Problem
- Proposition I: *A diagram says more than it is labelled to say.* Students see that two lines are
  parallel; the dataset only records what was marked. Show one real-style diagram with its marked
  relations in black and the unmarked-but-true ones appearing in red/yellow/blue.
  Key fact from Phase 3: asserted labels miss most true relations (fine-tunes score 0.54 against
  asserted labels vs 0.87 against geometric ones).
- Proposition II: *And sometimes says less.* Not-to-scale diagrams: a figure labelled
  perpendicular that is 12° off.
- Proposition III: *The Ruler.* The coordinate verifier and its tolerances, as an interactive
  figure: drag one line and watch the verdict flip at 3.7° (parallel/perpendicular); stretch a
  segment and watch equal-length flip at 10%. Margin notes: how the tolerances were calibrated
  (histogram valleys, two rounds of human gold labels).
- The task, stated formally: given an image and a highlighted query (two segments, a point and a
  circle, a segment and a circle, or a point and a line), predict which of six relations hold:
  parallel, perpendicular, equal_length, on_circle, tangent, collinear (none = all false).

### Book II: The Instrument
- The data: Geometry3K figures (1,296 train / 185 val / 370 test, split by figure), PGDP5K held-out
  source (660 figures), synthetic diagrams with exact labels (50k train, 1k extrapolation set with
  12-20 points). Draw the splits as a Byrne "partition of a square".
- The query: the highlight, drawn after resizing to 448 px (3 px core, 7 px halo). Show one diagram
  with four different queries side by side.
- The model, as an exploded figure: the 448-px image cut into a 28 × 28 grid of 16-px patches
  (784 tokens + CLS + 4 registers) → 24 transformer layers (DINOv3 ViT-L/16, 303M parameters,
  1024-d) → CLS ⊕ mean patch → LayerNorm → 6 sigmoids. Animate one patch token flowing up.
- The training recipe (sidenotes for detail): LP-FT (fit the head on frozen features of 20k queries,
  then fine-tune everything); 2 epochs × 40,000 images; layer-wise LR decay 0.8; bf16; EMA 0.999;
  relation-preserving augmentation only (rotations, flips, scale, noise; never shear). The compute
  story in one line: the plan needed ~1,400 GPU-hours, the project had a classroom PC for 2-3 hours
  and a Colab account, so every run got the same fixed budget.
- The two extensions: Part A (verifier as an unlimited labeller: synthetic data) and Part B
  (self-training on unlabelled real scans, filtered by deductive consistency).

### Book III: The Propositions (results)
Each finding is one proposition: a statement, a constructed figure, the numbers with seeds, Q.E.D.
All numbers come from the exported JSON (§5).

| # | Statement | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| IV | Fine-tuning reads geometry; a frozen eye does not | probe vs fine-tune bars per test set | 7.1, 7.2 |
| V | Colour speaks louder than letters | the same diagram twice: green highlight vs letter tokens; 0.87 vs 0.43 vs probe 0.60 | 7.8 |
| VI | A little synthetic geometry goes a long way, and then no further | scaling curve 0 / 1k / 10k / 50k on held-out and extrapolation | 7.9 |
| VII | The older teacher saw better | DINOv2-L vs DINOv3-L (one seed, labelled as such) | 7.5 |
| VIII | Resolution and augmentation | 224 vs 448, augmentation on/off | 7.6, 7.7 |
| IX | Consistency catches wrong labels, but more labels did not help | pseudo-label precision (0.882 → 0.898; removed 0.798) and the students vs the no-pseudo-label control | 7.10, 7.11 |
| X | Where it fails | per-relation F1; equal_length is the weak relation; (Phase 8: F1 vs verifier margin, attention maps) | Phase 8 |

Proposition IX is a negative result and must be told straight: the control without pseudo-labels
beat both filtered students. If the two extra control seeds are run before the site is built, use
the 3-seed numbers; otherwise say "one control seed".

## 3. The demo: three ways to try the model

The model cannot find points and lines by itself: primitive detection is out of scope (plan §0.2
#1). So in every mode the **visitor supplies the primitives** and asks about them. This is a feature
of the design, not a limitation to hide: "you point, it judges".

### 3.1 Construct (the flagship)
A compass-and-straightedge canvas in Byrne style.
- Tools: point (click), segment (click two points), circle (centre + a point on it), and "construct"
  helpers: parallel through a point, perpendicular through a point, midpoint, point on circle. Points
  get letters A, B, C… automatically; everything snaps to existing points.
- "Wobble" slider: adds a few degrees / percent of hand-drawn error, so the visitor can push a
  relation across the verifier's tolerance on purpose.
- **Ask:** select two segments (they turn red and yellow), or a point and a circle, etc., press Ask.
  The server renders the construction as a textbook diagram with the project's own synthetic
  renderer (same style as training), draws the green highlight with the project's overlay code, and
  returns the three verdicts:
  - **Ruler:** the verifier on the exact coordinates, with the measured angle / ratio and the
    tolerance ("2.1° off; parallel within 3.7°");
  - **Eye:** the model's probability per applicable relation;
  - the **model's view**: the 448-px input image with its green highlight.
- **Read the whole figure:** one button asks every query in the construction (the project's own
  query enumeration), then lists the model's relation set as Byrne-style statements ("AB ∥ CD",
  coloured by the shapes) next to the Ruler's, and runs the deductive closure
  (`verifier/consistency.py`) to flag contradictions in the model's answers. This is Part B, live.
  It needs a GPU backend to feel instant (§4.3); on CPU, show progress and cap at 30 queries.

### 3.2 Upload ("bring your own diagram")
- Drop or photograph a diagram (PNG/JPG, ≤ 5 MB, resized client-side to ≤ 1,600 px).
- Mark the primitives: click points (a magnifier loupe snaps to the nearest dark ink pixel), join
  them into segments, mark circles by centre and rim.
- Ask exactly as in Construct. There is no Ruler for uploads (clicked coordinates are approximate),
  but show "your clicks measure 88.6°" as a hint, clearly labelled as from the clicks, not the image.
- Honest framing on the panel: works best on clean printed textbook diagrams; photos of
  hand-drawn sketches are outside what it was trained on.
- Privacy: images are processed in memory and not stored; say so next to the upload box.

### 3.3 Beat the Transformer (the game)
Ten rounds. Each shows a diagram with two highlighted lines whose relation is **near the
tolerance** (e.g. 2.5°-5° from parallel). The visitor answers "parallel" or "not"; then the Ruler's
measurement and the model's probability are revealed. Final score card: you vs the model vs the
Ruler, shareable as an image.
- No server needed: the pool is **precomputed** from existing predictions. The synthetic
  extrapolation set (our own images, no licence questions) already has predictions for 10,000
  queries in `runs/<run>/predictions/synth_extrap.npz`, and the query index has the verifier margins,
  so the export script (§5) picks ~200 near-threshold items and writes images + answers.
- This mode keeps the site fun even when the inference server is asleep.

### 3.4 Gallery
Twelve precomputed diagrams (synthetic, plus real ones only if their licence allows, §7) with every
query already answered, so "Read the whole figure" is instant without a server. It is also the
fallback whenever the API is unreachable.

## 4. Architecture

```
web/
  site/            static front end (Vite + TypeScript, no UI framework needed; SVG + Canvas)
    public/data/   JSON + images written by web/export.py (generated, not hand-edited)
  server/          FastAPI inference service (Python), reuses data/, verifier/, models/ from the repo
  export.py        python -m web.export: runs/ + reports/ -> site/public/data/
tests/test_web_*.py
```

### 4.1 Parity rule (the most important engineering constraint)
The server must produce the model input with **the repository's own code**: `data/overlay.py`
(`flatten`, `draw_overlay` after `fit_long_side` to 448), `data/dataset.py` (padding to a multiple
of the patch size, normalisation), `data/synthetic/render.py` (Construct mode), `data/queries.py`
(enumeration, applicability masks), `verifier/checks.py` (Ruler) and `verifier/consistency.py`
(closure). Nothing about the model input is re-implemented in JavaScript; the browser only draws
previews. Test: for a few validation queries, the server's tensor equals `QueryDataset`'s tensor
for the same query exactly, and its probabilities match the run's saved `predictions/val.npz`
(tolerance 0.03, the bf16 CPU-vs-CUDA gap measured on 2026-10-13: 0 decisions differed).

User drawings become a `data.schema.Diagram` (needs a source name for user input; add one to
`SOURCES`, e.g. `"user"`, with a test).

### 4.2 API
```
POST /api/ask
  { "image": <png base64> | null, "construction": {points, segments, circles} | null,
    "primitives": {"points": {"A": [x, y], ...}, "segments": [["A","B"], ...],
                   "circles": [{"center": "O" | null, "center_xy": [x, y], "radius": r}]},
    "queries": [{"kind": "seg_pair", "refs": [["seg","A","B"], ["seg","C","D"]]}, ...] }
-> { "results": [{"refs": ..., "applicable": ["parallel", ...], "probs": {"parallel": 0.97, ...},
                  "ruler": {"parallel": {"holds": true, "margin": 2.1, "tolerance": 3.7}, ...} | null,
                  "model_input": <png base64>}],
     "model": "dinov2-l / ablation_dinov2_s0", "ms": 812 }
POST /api/read   same input without "queries": every query (cap 60, as in evaluation), plus
                 {"relations": [...], "ruler_relations": [...], "contradictions": [...]}
GET  /api/health -> {"ok": true, "model": ..., "device": "cpu"}
```
Limits: 5 MB image, 60 queries per request, simple per-IP rate limit; no storage of inputs.

### 4.3 Hosting
- **Front end:** static. Recommended: serve it from the same Hugging Face Space as the API (one URL,
  works while the GitHub repo is private). Alternatives: Cloudflare Pages or Netlify (free).
- **Inference:** a Hugging Face Space (Docker SDK, FastAPI). Cost of one query is one ViT-L forward
  pass at 448 px (~0.5 TFLOP). On the free CPU tier (2 vCPU) expect roughly 5-10 s per question:
  fine for single questions with a drawing animation while it thinks, too slow for "read the whole
  figure". For instant whole-figure reading use a GPU backend: Hugging Face ZeroGPU (needs a HF PRO
  account) or a serverless GPU (e.g. Modal, pay per second). Keep the backend behind one URL in the
  site's config so it can be switched without touching the front end.
- **Cold start:** free Spaces sleep when idle; the site pings `/api/health` on load, shows "the
  instrument is warming up" in Byrne style, and offers the Game and the Gallery meanwhile.

### 4.4 Which trained model serves the demo
Recommended: **`ablation_dinov2_s0`** (DINOv2-L, the best single model: 0.913 / 0.920 / 0.754 on
Geometry3K test / PGDP5K held-out / extrapolation; DINOv2 weights are Apache-2.0). Its `best.pt`
(bf16, ~0.6 GB) is on Google Drive at `geo-relations/runs/ablation_dinov2_s0/checkpoints/best.pt`;
the architecture builds from `Dinov2Config` without a gated download. Alternative:
`selftrain_control_s0` (DINOv3, 0.909 / 0.919 / 0.741), only after the DINOv3 licence check (§7).
Upload the checkpoint to a Hugging Face model repo the Space can read (private is fine).

## 5. Data export (`python -m web.export`)

Reads `runs/` and `reports/tables/` and writes `web/site/public/data/`:
- `results.json`: every experiment × unit × label set (mean, sd, seeds), from
  `analysis.phase7`'s functions, plus per-relation F1 for the headline models;
- `scaling.json`: the 7.9 points; `pseudo_labels.json`: the 7.11 precision table;
- `game.json` + `game/*.png`: ~200 near-threshold synthetic queries (image with highlight, verifier
  margin and tolerance, model probability from the demo model's saved predictions);
- `gallery.json` + `gallery/*.png`: twelve diagrams with every query answered;
- `facts.json`: dataset sizes, model facts (parameters, layers, tokens), tolerances, from configs
  and indexes.
Rerunning it after Phase 8 (CIs, attention maps) updates the page; no number is typed by hand. Test:
export on fake runs (as `tests/test_analysis_phase7.py` does) in under 10 s.

## 6. Milestones (each ends with something that works)

1. **Skeleton and export:** `web/export.py` with tests; Vite site that renders Books I-III as plain
   text from `results.json`. Check: every number on the page traces to a JSON field.
2. **The look:** design tokens, fonts, the book layout, margin notes, light and dark editions, phone
   layout. Check: 375 px wide without horizontal scroll; both themes.
3. **Constructions:** the SVG drawing engine (scroll-driven stroke animation) and the hero; then
   each proposition figure. Check: `prefers-reduced-motion` shows finished figures.
4. **Inference server:** FastAPI with `/api/ask`, `/api/read`, `/api/health`; parity test (§4.1);
   tiny-backbone tests; Dockerfile; local run with the real checkpoint on the Mac (MPS) first.
5. **Demo front end:** Construct, Upload, Game, Gallery; error and cold-start states.
6. **Deploy:** the Space (and static host if separate); check the deployed parity on three
   validation queries; latency measured and written in the colophon.
7. **Phase 8 hooks:** attention map overlay in "what the model sees", CIs on the result figures.

## 7. Licences and honesty (before anything is public)

- **DINOv3** weights are gated under Meta's DINOv3 licence: read it before serving a DINOv3-based
  fine-tune publicly (redistribution of derivatives, attribution, acceptable use). The recommended
  DINOv2 model avoids the question (Apache-2.0).
- **Dataset images** (Geometry3K, PGDP5K, PGPS9K): check each licence before showing real
  diagrams on the page or in the gallery; until then use synthetic diagrams only (ours).
- The page states what the model cannot do (no primitive detection; trained on clean textbook
  diagrams) and labels single-seed results and the small not-to-scale subset as such.
- Uploaded images are not stored or logged.

## 8. Decisions for the user before building

1. **Demo model:** `ablation_dinov2_s0` (recommended) or a DINOv3 model after the licence check.
2. **Inference budget:** free CPU Space (single questions, ~5-10 s each) or a GPU backend
   (instant "read the whole figure"; HF PRO for ZeroGPU, or pay-per-use serverless).
3. **Part B numbers:** run the two extra control seeds first (recommended) or show one seed.
4. **Timing:** build now from Phase 7 data and add Phase 8 later (recommended), or wait.
5. **Name on the page** and whether the GitHub repository becomes public (links in the colophon).
